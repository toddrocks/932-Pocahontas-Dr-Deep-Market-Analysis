01
The Approach

There is a version of this analysis that runs forty adjustments across a spreadsheet and produces a number to the dollar. It looks impressive and it hides a problem: every one of those adjustments runs through the same handful of assumptions, so if one assumption is off, the whole thing is off in the same direction and nothing in the output warns you.

So this was built the opposite way. Four simple methods, each answering the same question from a completely different starting point, none of them sharing assumptions with the others.

If four independent approaches land in the same place, that agreement means something. If they scatter, that tells you something too.

They landed within fifty thousand dollars of each other.

THE FOUR QUESTIONS
Where does this property sit among what has actually sold? What is the land worth, plus what sits on it? Where does it fall between the two sales that bracket it? And what do renovated homes on this water actually trade for?

02
What Drives Waterfront Value Here

SIZE
Square footage stops paying above roughly 2,600 feet.
One recent sale added more than 3,000 square feet and a pool over a comparable property and gained almost nothing for it. At 2,347 square feet, your home is not penalized for its size. It sits right at the top of what this market pays for.

FRONTAGE
Waterfront feet are worth far more at the beginning than at the end.
The first fifty feet buys everything that matters: the water, the view, the dock, the address. Every foot after that returns less.

The market has already proven this twice this year. Two Cinco Bayou properties with roughly double your frontage sold in 2026 for $825,000 and $775,000, both well beneath the price recommended here. Frontage is real and it has been accounted for. It is not what sets the price.

LAND
Land carries the value.
Cinco Bayou frontage is finite. Your 0.85 acres, confirmed against county records, is larger than nearly every waterfront parcel that has sold here. The parcel runs 436 feet deep and widens toward the road, which places the home well back with mature tree cover on both sides. Buyers feel the privacy before they ever reach the water.

WATER ASSETS
The water is a set of assets on its own.
Of the comparable sales behind this analysis, one has a dock but no lift or boathouse, and one has no boating infrastructure at all. Your property has deep water, a permitted dock with boathouse and lift, and a sound seawall. Because Cinco Bayou sits outside any aquatic preserve and your dock structure is permitted and documented, that package transfers cleanly rather than becoming a question in underwriting.

It also carries a transferable flood policy at $2,677 a year. As federal flood rates continue climbing, a legacy assumable policy is real money to a buyer every year they own the home. The prior listing never presented it.

04
The Market Since 2024

The Emerald Coast has softened modestly over the past two years. Waterfront has held up better than the broader market, which is what you would expect where supply is physically fixed.

The change is in leverage rather than price. In 2024, waterfront here regularly sold at or above asking. Nothing has sold above asking in 2026.

What has not changed is speed. Correctly priced waterfront still reaches contract in under sixty days. The three homes currently competing on Cinco Bayou have been listed between four and six months, have collectively cut their prices by roughly a quarter, and none is under contract.

The pattern is binary. Property either moves quickly or it does not move at all.

What Happened With the Last Listing

Listed May 2024 at $1,189,000 and cancelled 122 days later.

The price did generate real interest. Buyers engaged at that number, which tells us the property has genuine appeal at a strong price and that the pricing was not wildly out of step with the market as it stood in 2024. What was missing was everything around it.

The price never adjusted.
Across four months the price never moved, and there was no strategy in place to convert the interest it drew or to respond to what the market was signaling.

The property's real advantages were never presented.
The parcel was published with an incorrect road frontage that made the site look far smaller than it is. The transferable flood policy never reached buyers. The covered lanai and outdoor kitchen, the strongest feature of this property, was not the centerpiece of the marketing.

Why $1,189,000 Is Not the Number Today

01
That interest came in a stronger market.
In 2024 waterfront here regularly sold at or above asking. That is no longer the case. The same buyer interest today supports a number below where it landed two years ago.

02
It sits at the edge of a two-year segment record.
Asking $1,189,000 places this home within five percent of the highest price ever paid for waterfront in Ocean City, which was set by a renovated home with double your frontage and no flood insurance requirement.

03
Two homes are already stuck $89,000 beneath it.
Two waterfront listings are sitting unsold at $1,100,000 right now, one of them for 163 days. The market is currently declining property well below the prior asking price. Testing $1,189,000 against that is not an ambitious price. It is a price with no observed path.

04
The cost of testing a high price is not recoverable.
A listing draws its largest and most qualified audience in its first three weeks. Priced beyond the market, that audience passes and does not come back when the price is corrected later. That is exactly what happened in 2024, and the 122 days already on record make the risk sharper the second time, not softer.

How This Property Gets Marketed

The last listing was not defeated by price alone. It was defeated by presentation. Three specific failures are corrected before this property goes live.

WHAT WENT WRONG · WHAT CHANGES

The parcel was published showing 12 feet of road frontage instead of 122.5, which made an 0.85 acre estate parcel read as a sliver.
Corrected dimensions, verified acreage, and the county parcel map included in the listing file from day one.

The transferable flood policy at $2,677 appeared only in a note to other agents and never reached a single buyer.
The policy, its transferability, and the cost difference against a new policy are stated in the public remarks and documented in the disclosure package.

The covered lanai and outdoor kitchen, the strongest feature of this property, was buried in the photo order.
It becomes the lead image and the organizing idea of the entire campaign.

THE DELIVERABLES

Photography and video.
Professional interior and exterior photography, twilight exterior session, drone footage capturing the cove position, the depth of the parcel, and the dock, boathouse and lift from the water. Walkthrough video and floor plan.

A dedicated property website.
The property gets its own site rather than a syndicated listing page, built around the water and the outdoor living, with the disclosure package accessible directly from it.

Full syndication and paid placement.
MLS, Zillow, Realtor.com, Homes.com, and brokerage syndication, supported by targeted paid distribution to buyers searching Emerald Coast waterfront and to relocation traffic from Eglin and Hurlburt.

Direct agent outreach.
Personal outreach to every agent who has shown or listed Cinco Bayou waterfront in the past twenty four months, plus the agents currently holding buyers in the $900,000 to $1,200,000 waterfront band.

Broker and neighbor preview.
A broker open before public launch and a neighbor preview, because on this stretch of the bayou the next owner is frequently someone who already knows the water.

The Strategy

PRICE
List at $1,100,000.
Modestly above the indicated value of $1,060,000, preserving the negotiating room these transactions actually settle on. Level with two stalled competitors this property clearly outperforms, and arriving fresh against inventory buyers have already passed over. Visible to buyers searching in both directions from $1.1 million.

THE PRICE ADJUSTMENT PLAN
Both numbers are agreed before we go live.
Your frustration with the last listing was that the price sat still for four months. It will not happen again, and the way to guarantee that is to decide the triggers now, while nothing is at stake, rather than in a difficult conversation at day 60.

Day 14 review. If the listing has produced fewer than six showings and no written offer, the price moves to $1,075,000.

Day 30 review. If cumulative showings remain under twelve and no written offer has been received, the price moves to $1,050,000.

Both figures are round-number search thresholds, so each adjustment moves the property into a new pool of buyers rather than simply lowering it within the same one. Neither adjustment is automatic if the activity thresholds are met. Strong showing traffic without an offer signals a different problem than no traffic at all, and we address the actual problem rather than reflexively cutting.

TERMS
Stated plainly, so nothing has to be reverse-engineered.
Listing agreement: six months, exclusive right of sale.
Seller-side brokerage fee: 3% of the sale price.
Buyer-side compensation: negotiated on each offer, typically 2% to 3%, and reflected in the net sheets above.
All brokerage fees are fully negotiable and are not set by law or by any brokerage or association.

DILIGENCE
Answer the buyer's questions before they ask.
Survey, elevation certificate, flood policy and transfer confirmation, dock and lift permits, seawall documentation, roof age, and a projected tax estimate, all available at first showing. The same package goes to the appraiser. Waterfront deals collapse in underwriting when these surface late. This one will not.

RESPONSE
Work the offer, not just the listing.
Activity and feedback reviewed weekly with competing listings monitored. And when an offer arrives it is worked deliberately, with communication managed on both sides so a willing buyer reaches the closing table.
