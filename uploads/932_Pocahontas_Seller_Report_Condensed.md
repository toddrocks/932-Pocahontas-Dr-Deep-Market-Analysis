## The Approach

Averaging price per square foot works on ordinary homes. On waterfront it produces a number that no buyer's agent will accept and no appraiser will support.

The proof is in the local data. Among recent Cinco Bayou sales, the smallest home traded at the highest price per square foot and the largest traded at the lowest. A 2023 build sold for less per foot than a 2012 build. Square footage is simply not what this market prices.

So this analysis was built from every waterfront sale on Cinco Bayou over the past two years, examined individually and adjusted line by line against your property. Three separate comparables, adjusted independently, landed within $25,000 of each other.

That agreement is what gives the recommendation its weight.

---

## What Actually Drives Waterfront Value Here

**Square footage stops paying above roughly 2,600 feet.** One recent sale added more than 3,000 square feet and a pool over a comparable property and gained almost nothing for it. At 2,347 square feet, your home is not penalized for its size. It sits right at the top of what this market pays for.

**Waterfront feet are worth far more at the beginning than at the end.** The first fifty feet buys everything that matters: the water, the view, the dock, the address. Past ninety feet, additional frontage is nearly free. Two properties with more than double your frontage sold this year for well under the price recommended here.

Your 53 feet is real and it has been fully accounted for. But a straight proportional calculation would penalize this property far more severely than the market actually does, and the adjustment used here follows the real curve.

**Land carries the value.** Cinco Bayou frontage is finite. Your 0.85 acres, confirmed against county records, is larger than nearly every waterfront parcel that has sold here. The parcel runs 436 feet deep and widens toward the road, which places the home well back with mature tree cover on both sides. Buyers feel the privacy before they ever reach the water.

**The water is a set of assets on its own.** Of the three most comparable recent sales, one has a dock but no lift or boathouse, and one has no boating infrastructure at all. Your property has deep water, a permitted dock with boathouse and lift, and a sound seawall.

It also carries a transferable flood policy at $2,677 a year. As federal flood rates continue climbing, a legacy assumable policy is real money to a buyer every year they own the home. The prior listing never presented it.

## What the Market Has Done Since 2024

The Emerald Coast has softened modestly over the past two years. Waterfront has held up better than the broader market, which is what you would expect where supply is physically fixed.

The bigger change is in leverage. In 2024, waterfront here regularly sold at or above asking. Nothing has sold above asking in 2026. Values are off modestly, concentrated at the upper end.

What has not changed is speed. Correctly priced waterfront still reaches contract in under sixty days. The three homes currently competing on Cinco Bayou have been listed between four and six months, have collectively cut their prices by roughly a quarter, and none is under contract.

The pattern is binary. Property either moves quickly or it does not move at all.

## Why the Last Listing Did Not Sell

Listed May 2024 at $1,189,000. Cancelled 122 days later.

**The price belonged to a different market segment.** It sat within five percent of the all-time ceiling for Ocean City waterfront, on the narrowest frontage in the entire comparable set.

**The price never moved.** Not once in four months. The market gave continuous feedback and the listing did not respond to any of it.

**The property's real advantages were never presented.** The parcel was published with an incorrect road frontage that made the site look far smaller than it is. The flood policy never reached buyers. The covered lanai and outdoor kitchen, the strongest feature of this property, was not the centerpiece of the marketing.

An offer came close and did not close. That is a useful data point, but it was made two years ago in a stronger market and it is not a floor.

## Why Returning to That Price Would Repeat the Result

The instinct to go back to $1,189,000 is natural. Four things argue against it.

**That offer was made in a stronger market.** Time-adjusted, the same buyer behavior today supports something closer to $1,120,000, before accounting for the fact that it never closed.

**No property in this segment has sold there.** Asking $1,189,000 places this home within five percent of a segment record while carrying the least frontage in the data.

**We already know what happens at that price.** Two waterfront homes are sitting unsold at $1,100,000 right now, one with roughly twice your frontage. If the market is declining those, there is no path above them.

**The cost of testing a high price is not recoverable.** A listing draws its largest and most qualified audience in its first three weeks. Priced beyond the market, that audience passes and does not come back when the price is corrected later. That is exactly what happened in 2024, and the 122 days already on record make the risk sharper the second time, not softer.

## Why $1,100,000 and Not $1,099,000

This looks like a small distinction. It is one of the few decisions that costs nothing and returns real exposure.

Buyers find homes through search filters, and filters run on round numbers. Someone caps their search at $1.1 million. Someone else starts at $1.1 million and searches upward. A listing at $1,100,000 appears in both. A listing at $1,099,000 appears only in the first, and is invisible to every buyer whose search starts at $1.1 million.

Pricing just under a round number is a retail tactic built for shelf tags. It has no application where discovery happens through a database query.

## The Strategy

**List at $1,100,000.** Modestly above the indicated value, preserving the negotiating room these transactions actually settle on. Level with two stalled competitors this property clearly outperforms. Visible to buyers searching in both directions from $1.1 million. And well beneath a price the market has already declined.

**Lead with the water and the outdoor living.** Professional photography, twilight work, and drone footage capturing the cove, the depth of the parcel, and the dock. Correct acreage and dimensions in the listing from day one.

**Answer the buyer's questions before they ask.** Survey, elevation certificate, flood policy and transfer confirmation, dock and lift permits, seawall documentation, roof age, and a projected tax estimate, all available at first showing. Waterfront deals collapse in underwriting when these surface late. This one will not.

**Respond to the market.** Activity and feedback reviewed at two weeks and four weeks against a plan agreed in advance, with competing listings monitored weekly. This property will not sit at an unresponsive price for four months again.

The last listing did not fail for lack of a buyer. It failed because it was priced against a market this property does not compete in, then held there while the best weeks of exposure passed.

Priced correctly, waterfront on Cinco Bayou goes under contract in under sixty days. Priced incorrectly, it does not sell at all. Two listings are demonstrating that right now.

At $1,100,000 this property enters fresh, at the price where qualified buyers are actively searching, with the strongest feature set in that band, and with every question a buyer or their lender will ask already answered.
